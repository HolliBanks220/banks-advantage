Log it honestly

Four outcomes, and every call is one of them. Appt set when you book it. Callback when they name a time. Not interested when they are done. No answer for everything else — including a conversation that went nowhere.

Spoke to them but they would not name a time? That is still No answer. Use Callback without a time only when you want them back sooner than the queue would bring them.

Not interested is final. It closes their case, clears any appointment and takes them out of your queue. Right button when they mean it, wrong one when they are just busy. That is what Callback is for.

The appointment

Sitting down, through to the application being in.

Log the outcome from the appointment

Open the lead → Log outcome. Six choices, and they mean different things:

Sale · No sale — you sat and showed numbers.
Continuation — you sat, and you are going back. Policy pickup, think about it, a rewrite.
Sat — didn't show numbers — you sat and never presented. No need, already covered, could not present.
Cancelled — still live — they called ahead. Case stays open, back into your queue.
Cancelled — not interested — they called to say they are done.

Log it here, not from the business list. Logging the outcome is what brings up the referral, reset and recruit questions — and you want those while you are still with them.
Cancelled is not a no sale. If they never saw numbers there was never really an appointment. Logging it as a no sale makes your close ratio a lie. And sat — didn't show numbers stays out of close ratio entirely: you cannot fail to close something you never presented.

Ask, while you are still sitting there

You get these questions twice. The moment you log a sale or a no sale, one screen opens: referrals, resets, recruits. That is before you enter any applications, on purpose — do the asking while you are still in the room.

Then you enter the applications. Then the same screen comes back, for anyone who came up while you were writing.

While you are in there, write what the coverage does for them. "Six months of mortgage payments." That one line is what makes every follow-up call easy — at 90 days, at 8 months, at the review — because you are not ringing to check in, you are ringing about the thing you built for them.
Yes, after a no sale too. They did not buy. They still know people, and they have just spent an hour with you. The ask costs nothing.

The money works differently

Amount is the account moving — the money itself, not your revenue. APV is 5% of that and is reporting only.

Your commission is your rate times the account amount, never times APV. Insure Realty runs its own ladder, 3.5% at 80 up to 9% at 130.

Only Insure Realty or an annuity counts as retirement. A life policy written on an Advanced Market case is still a life policy and is priced as one. And if you leave the account amount blank, nothing is counted — no account, no number.

Track it through

The Business tab is every application you have written and where each one stands.

Move the status as the carrier does

Written → Submitted → Approved → Issued → Paid. Update it in Business as it moves.

Three that are not the ladder: Declined — the carrier said no. Not taken — the carrier said yes and the client walked, usually over the rating. Lapse / Cancel — it was in force and stopped.

Written earns nothing. It has not reached the carrier. Your commission figures start counting at Submitted, which is the honest place to start.
Not taken still counts as a sale and still counts in your submitted APV — you closed them, and it is on your record either way. It drags your net placed ratio down, because it should.

If a policy lapses

Set it to Lapse or Cancel. The field is Chargeback $ and it wants annual premium, not commission — how much of the APV the carrier clawed back.

Leave it blank if they took all of it. That is the normal case, and blank means the whole premium.

You keep whatever is left. $1,000 of premium with $400 clawed back leaves $600, and your commission is worked out on that. Take back all of it and it earns nothing.

Keep showing up

After delivery the app spaces the rest out for you: 90 days a check-in, 8 months another, 13 months the proper review, plus birthdays.

Stuck?

The things that catch everyone. Search at the top of the page finds these too.

A lead disappeared and I can't find them

Search finds leads in any status, including dead ones — type any part of the name or number in the Leads search box. It also matches the name on an application, so a spouse is findable too.

If they're gone because you logged something by mistake, use Settings → History. Pick today and the hour, find the action, tap Undo this. The lead comes back exactly as it was, appointment and all.

The ↺ icon next to the gear undoes the very last thing you did, if it was just now.

I logged Not interested by mistake

Open the lead, find that line in the call log, and remove it. Taking the entry away puts the lead back the way it was — case reopened, appointment restored, status back to what it was. See logging outcomes.

I sold a policy but I can't enter the application

The + Log outcome button only appears on an open case. If every case on that lead is closed, there's nothing for the application to attach to.

Book the appointment again and you'll be offered Reopen a case — it lists every closed case so you can pick the right one. It goes back to open and you can log the sit and the application. See the appointment section.

What's the difference between a reset and a continuation?

A continuation is the same case, next meeting — you sat, and you're going back. Policy pickup, a rewrite, a think-about-it.

A reset is a different product. They said no to mortgage protection but they'll talk about retirement. That opens a new case, and it doesn't undo the first verdict.

They cancelled but want to reschedule — what do I log?

Cancelled — still live. The case stays open, the appointment clears, and they go straight back into your call queue. You'll be offered a callback or a rebook right after.

Don't use no sale — that means they saw your numbers and passed.

An old client is showing an appointment that shouldn't be there

Settings → Repair. It lists anyone in a contradictory state — an appointment on a lead who said no, a live step on a closed case, a callback on someone with nothing open.

It shows what's wrong before it changes anything, and it never touches sales, sits or logged outcomes.

Why did my client disappear from the call list this evening?

Calling curfew. It's by their local time, and by age: 70 and over stops at 7pm, 65 to 69 at 8pm, everyone else at 9:30. Their card reads Too late to call.

An appointment you already booked still shows. It's cold calling that stops.

My commission per lead looks far too high or too low

Check your contract level first — Settings → Profile. Every figure is premium × that number.

Then tap the commission figure in Reports. It lists every application behind it, biggest first, with what each one contributed. One wrong application is usually the whole gap.

Why does a month show numbers from the month before?

It doesn't — you're looking at a production month. July runs from the Saturday after June's last Friday through July's last Friday. A charge dated June 30 lands in July because that's the carrier's July.

What does N/A mean in a report?

No data for that period — usually a period before you started using CWC. It's deliberately not a zero, because zero would read as "you did nothing" rather than "nothing was recorded".

Will I lose everything if I clear my browser?

No. Leads, calls, appointments, applications and settings all live in the database. Log in from another machine and it's all there.

What counts as a sit?

You showed them numbers. That's it. One per case, whatever it took to get there — so a lead you met three times before they decided is still one sit.

A cancellation isn't a sit. A no-show isn't a sit.

Should I be looking at which lead types perform best?

Not yet, and it isn't turned on for you. New agents who study lead quality learn to blame the leads. Learn to work them first — the numbers will still be there in three months, and they'll mean more.
